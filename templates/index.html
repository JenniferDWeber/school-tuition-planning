What changes in {{ cfg.first_credit_year }}

  • Donors get the money back. From January 1, {{ cfg.first_credit_year }}, a person who gives to a scholarship granting organization (SGO) can take up to ${{ '{:,}'.format(cfg.credit_max_individual) }} off their federal taxes, dollar for dollar. Any state tax credit for the same gift is subtracted first.
  • Only in states that opt in. A donor can live anywhere, but the SGO must be located in, and give scholarships to students living in, a state that has opted in and sent the IRS its list of SGOs by {{ cfg.sgo_list_deadline }}.
  • Many families qualify. Students qualify if their household income is no more than {{ cfg.income_limit_pct_amgi }}% of the area median, adjusted for family size. Scholarships can pay private school tuition and other education costs.
  • Schools can't steer the money. SGOs may not earmark donations for a particular student. Families apply to the SGO, and the SGO decides on awards. The school can encourage giving, but not direct it.
  • Where the family lives counts, not where the school is. A school near a state line may enroll students from a state that has not opted in. Those students can't receive these scholarships.

Treasury's rules were proposed on October 1, 2026, and are open for comment, so details may change. A school should confirm specifics with its diocese and tax adviser.

Tuition planner

Use this to test next year's tuition before you announce it: what it does to families, to your budget, and to your enrollment, with federal scholarships in the picture.

How to use it

  1. Replace the example numbers with your own. The boxes start with a sample school of 250 students. Change "Your school" first. The other sections have sensible starting values you can adjust later.
  2. Read the suggested tuition. It's the price that balances next year's budget without losing more students than you said you can afford. If no price does both, it tells you how much is still missing.
  3. Review the proposed tuition. It updates automatically to the suggested tuition as you change your inputs. You may also set it manually with the slider to test other prices. The cards underneath show what a family with 1, 2 or 3 children would pay, how your budget comes out, and how much scholarship money is involved.
  4. Check the five-year outlook. A budget that balances next year can still slip into a gap as costs rise and enrollment falls. The table shows when.

Nothing you type is saved or sent anywhere. It stays in your browser. All results are estimates to plan with, not a guarantee.

If your state hasn't opted in

{{ meta.states_not_in }} states (counting DC) have not opted in yet. Their Catholic schools teach {{ '{:,}'.format(meta.catholic_students_not_in) }} students. Schools there still have options:

  • It isn't too late. States decide year by year. For {{ meta.irs_year }}, a state must opt in by January 1, {{ meta.irs_year }} and send its SGO list by {{ cfg.sgo_list_deadline }}. A state that misses that can still join for later years.
  • Families across the state line still qualify. {{ meta.not_in_with_opted_neighbor }} of these states border one that has opted in. A student who lives in an opted-in state can receive a scholarship even if the school is across the line. Enter that share in the planner.
  • Your state's donors can still claim the credit, but the money leaves the state. They can only give to SGOs in opted-in states, so those dollars help other states' families, not yours. That is a strong point to make to state leaders.
  • Show leaders the numbers. Use the planner's "if my state opts in" option to show what scholarships would mean for your families, and work with your state Catholic conference and diocese.
  • Plan with the aid you have now. Enter state programs, diocesan tuition funds and parish support under "Other outside aid" so the outlook reflects today, not a hoped-for change.
